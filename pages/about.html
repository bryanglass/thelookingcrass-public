About

Experience changes the way you see systems.

The Looking Crass comes from working inside complicated systems, not merely studying them from a comfortable distance.

The perspective

Work teaches you where the story breaks.

Restaurants, hospitality, enterprise operations, technology, teams, budgets, customers, and processes all reveal the same basic truth: the documented system and the lived system are rarely identical.

That difference is where useful observation begins.

The work behind The Looking Crass combines hands-on hospitality experience with enterprise operational leadership, systems thinking, process analysis, technology, and emerging AI practice.

The goal is not to make complicated things sound impressive. It is to make them understandable enough to improve.

Background

A deliberately mixed toolkit.

The combination is unusual. That is useful.

Hospitality

Frontline reality

Restaurant, banquet, kitchen, service, training, food safety, guest experience, labor awareness, and the operational details that customers actually experience.

Operations

Systems under pressure

Enterprise and municipal operations, cross-functional coordination, documentation, budgets, service delivery, readiness, and team development.

Technology

Systems thinking

Infrastructure, data protection, workflow design, operational tooling, and the practical question of whether technology is actually solving the problem.

Intelligence

Evidence before narrative

Public information, operational data, customer feedback, and structured analysis used to find patterns rather than confirm assumptions.

AI

Useful augmentation

AI explored as a practical analytical and operational capability, with attention to evidence, human review, responsible use, and actual utility.

Building

From observation to artifact

When the answer should exist as a workflow, database, dashboard, model, document, or application, the thinking has somewhere concrete to go.

A working principle

The point is not to be right about the story. The point is to find out whether the story survives contact with reality.
