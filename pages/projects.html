Projects

Ideas are cheap. Artifacts are more interesting.

The work below represents practical investigations, frameworks, prototypes, and operating concepts. Each starts with a problem worth examining.

Glassware

Hospitality orchestration

The problem.
Event information is often written for sales and contractual completeness, while frontline teams need something very different: clear operational instructions for the work they are about to perform.

The approach.
Translate complex Banquet Event Order information into role-specific operational views for management, culinary, kitchen prep, captains, service staff, greeters, and other operational roles.

Status: demonstrated workflow and prototype concept; continuing development.

Guest Intelligence Platform

Operational intelligence

The problem.
Guest feedback is abundant but often remains trapped as isolated reviews, comments, or anecdotes rather than becoming structured operational intelligence.

The approach.
Organize public review information and other available signals into a searchable corpus that can support trend analysis, recurring-issue detection, comparative intelligence, and operational questions.

Status: framework and development concept.

CapabilityDB

Capability intelligence

The problem.
Resumes and profiles describe people in prose, but prose is a poor database.

The approach.
Structure capabilities, evidence, relationships, maturity, and consumer-facing representations into a system that can support search, analysis, and controlled public release.

Status: active system development.

Business Discovery

Field research

The problem.
A business can look very different depending on whether you read its website, reviews, menu, job postings, public records, or customer comments.

The approach.
Combine public information and structured observation into an executive picture of what a business does, how it presents itself, where friction may exist, and where capabilities may align with its needs.

Status: repeatable discovery workflow.

Hospitality Operations Analysis

Field operations

The problem.
Hospitality operations generate a constant stream of information about labor, service, guest experience, food, workflow, and readiness. Much of it disappears at the end of the shift.

The approach.
Build practical models, routines, training paths, and operational documentation that turn observations into repeatable improvements.

Status: active practical work.
